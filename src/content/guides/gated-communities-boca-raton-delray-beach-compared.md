Drive around Boca Raton and Delray Beach for one afternoon and you'll notice something: a huge share of the homes here sit behind a gate. Gated communities are the default in this part of Florida, not the exception — but "gated community" covers everything from a small neighborhood with a gate and a pool to a private city-within-a-city with its own golf courses, marina, and social calendar. This page breaks down the types, so you can figure out which kind — if any — is actually built for your life.

### The main types

**Golf and country club communities.** The house comes with a club: golf, tennis, pickleball, pools, a clubhouse with restaurants and events. In many of these communities, joining the club is required when you buy, with an upfront membership cost and ongoing dues on top of the association fees. For the right buyer this is the best deal in town — your whole social life and your weekends in one place. For the wrong buyer, it's paying every month for a golf course you'll never set foot on. Be honest with yourself about which one you are.

**Everyday communities.** Gated neighborhoods built around daily amenities: a clubhouse, a pool, maybe sports courts and a playground, but no mandatory club membership. Fees are generally lower than the country club communities, the homes are often newer or bigger for the money, and a lot of them sit in west Boca and west Delray. If you want the gate and the amenities without the country club bill, this is often the better fit.

**Active adult communities.** A major category out here, especially in west Delray. These are built around activities — clubs, classes, shows, sports leagues, a full-time social calendar — and around easy living: smaller yards, single-story homes, and maintenance handled by the association. Occupancy rules are set by the community and can affect both daily life and resale, so read them carefully before you buy.

**Luxury and estate communities.** Guard-gated, large lots, custom homes, sometimes a marina or beach club attached. The gate here is about privacy as much as security, the fees reflect the staff and the grounds, and the homes are destinations in themselves. Boca Raton has some of the most famous of these in the country.

**Small gated neighborhoods.** Not every gate comes with a lifestyle. Plenty of smaller communities offer a gate, a pool, and not much else — and charge accordingly. If all you want is a quiet street and a little extra privacy, these are often the best value in the whole category.

### What to weigh

**The full monthly cost.** The mortgage is only part of it. Add the association fees, the club dues if there are any, and the upfront membership or application costs when you buy. Compare communities on the full number, not the listing price — two homes at the same price can cost very different amounts to own.

**The rules.** Associations have them, and they are enforced: what you can park in the driveway, whether you can rent your home out and how soon after buying, pet limits, guest rules, approval processes for changes to the house. None of this is hidden — it's all in the community documents — but you have to actually read them before you buy, not after the first violation letter.

**Renting restrictions, if you might ever rent it out.** Many communities require you to own the home for a period before renting, limit rentals to once a year, or set minimum lease terms. If renting the home out someday is part of your plan — seasonal income, or a trial run before selling — check the rules now. This is the single most common unpleasant surprise I see.

**Location versus amenities.** The farther west you go, the more community you get for your money — and the farther you are from the beach and the downtowns. Neither is better. But a clubhouse you'll use every day beats a beach you'll drive to twice a month, and the reverse is also true. Know your actual habits, not your vacation habits.

**The association itself.** A well-run association with healthy reserves keeps the place up and avoids nasty surprise assessments. One that's been putting off maintenance will collect the money eventually — possibly from you. Part of buying in any community is looking at the health of the association, not just the house.

### The bottom line

There is no best community in Boca or Delray — there's the one that matches how you live and what you want your month to cost. The fastest way to sort it out is to tell somebody who works here how you actually live: golf or no golf, a lively club calendar or a quieter setting, beach every day or beach on weekends, rent it out someday or never. The list gets short very fast from there.
