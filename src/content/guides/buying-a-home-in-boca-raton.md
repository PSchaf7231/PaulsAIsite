Boca Raton is not one market. It's a stack of very different neighborhoods and communities sitting under one name, and the price of the name itself is part of what you're buying. This page won't tell you which neighborhood is best — that's a conversation, not a paragraph — but it will tell you how the place is laid out and how buying here actually works.

### The lay of the land

**East Boca** is the older, closer-in part of town — nearer to the beach, the Intracoastal, and Mizner Park and downtown. You'll find everything from original ranch homes on regular streets to some of the most expensive waterfront property in Florida. Land is a bigger part of the price here, and many homes have been torn down and rebuilt.

**Central Boca** is the heart of the suburban city: master-planned communities built from the 1970s onward, many of them gated, many built around golf courses, lakes, and country clubs. This is where a lot of families land, because the communities come with the amenities built in and the schools people move here for.

**West Boca** is newer and more spread out — bigger communities, newer construction, more house for the money than the east side, and a longer drive to the beach. For a lot of buyers coming from the Northeast, west Boca is where the math works: a newer home, a gated community, and a price that feels sane compared to what they sold up north.

Sprinkled through all of it are country club communities where buying the house also means joining the club — sometimes with a substantial upfront membership and ongoing dues on top of the association fees. For golfers and people who want the social life built in, that's the whole point. For everyone else, it's a cost to understand before you fall for the house.

### How buying works in Florida

Florida does a few things differently than New York and New Jersey, and the differences matter.

**No attorney review period the way you're used to.** Up north, contracts often go through an attorney review window after signing. Here, the standard contract is signed and binding much faster, with the protections built into the contract itself — inspection periods, financing terms, and deadlines written right into the document. Read it before you sign it, because once you sign, the clock is running.

**The inspection period is your window.** After you're under contract, you typically have a set number of days to inspect the home and decide. In that window you're looking at the roof, the air conditioning, the plumbing and electric, and — on older homes — things like cast iron pipes and older panels that can affect insurance. What you find in that window decides whether you move forward, negotiate, or walk.

**Insurance comes early, not at the end.** On a Florida home, getting a bindable insurance quote can make or break a closing, especially on an older roof. Smart buyers check insurability during the inspection period, not the week before closing.

**Associations approve buyers too.** If the home is in an association community — and in Boca, most are — the association may require an application and approval before you can close. It's usually straightforward for a buyer, but it takes time, and it belongs on the calendar from day one.

**Closing costs and homestead.** Florida charges documentary stamp taxes and title-related costs that differ from what you paid up north. And once the home is your primary residence, filing for the [Florida homestead exemption](/guides/what-it-costs-to-live-in-palm-beach-county) lowers your property tax bill and caps how fast your assessed value can rise year to year. It's one of the first things to do after you close, and it has a filing deadline each year, so don't let it slide.

### What I'd tell a friend buying in Boca

Decide your community type before you decide your house. A great house in the wrong kind of community — a club you'll never use, an association whose rules you'll fight, a drive to the beach you swore you'd make and won't — is the wrong house. Get clear on east versus west, club versus no club, gated versus open neighborhood, and the list of homes to look at gets short and manageable fast.

Then be ready. The homes that are priced right in the neighborhoods everybody wants do not sit around waiting for a second look. Having your financing lined up — a real pre-approval, or proof of funds if you're paying cash — is the difference between getting the house and hearing about who did.
