Your buyer might be a relocating family from New Jersey, a retiree downsizing from a bigger house across town, a seasonal renter who fell in love with the building, or an investor — and the way you prepare, price, and time the sale should account for all of them. ### Preparation: fix what a buyer will find anyway

Buyers here look at a lot of homes, and they compare yours against fresh, clean competition — including brand-new construction out west. You don't need to remodel your house to sell it. You do need to handle the things that scare buyers off or give them a reason to chip at the price.

Start with the obvious: deep clean, declutter, and take care of the deferred maintenance that shows — the dripping faucet, the cracked tiles, the door that sticks. Then think about the Florida items, because buyers and their inspectors will: the age and condition of the roof, the air conditioning, and the water heater. On an older home, the roof is often the whole conversation, because insurance companies care deeply about roof age here, and a roof near the end of its life can limit your buyer pool to people who don't need financing or insurance the usual way. Knowing where you stand on those items before you list — and pricing with them in mind — beats finding out from a buyer's inspection report.

Presentation matters more than most sellers expect. Good photos, taken in good light, of clean and uncluttered rooms, are the front door of your listing. Most buyers will decide whether to see your home from their phone. If the photos don't earn the visit, nothing else gets the chance.

### Pricing: the market decides, and it decides fast

The most common mistake I see is pricing for the home the seller remembers instead of the one the buyer is comparing. Buyers have the same information you do — they can see what similar homes sold for, and how long the overpriced ones have been sitting. In this market, a home that comes out at the right price gets attention in the first two weeks, when the most buyers are watching. A home that starts high tends to sit, and a home that sits collects questions: what's wrong with it, why hasn't it sold, how much will they take now?

Pricing right doesn't mean pricing low. It means pricing where the recent comparable sales support you, with your home's real condition — roof, updates, location inside or outside a community — honestly counted. I'd rather defend a strong price with good comparables than explain a stale listing. And when we do talk price, I'll show you the same numbers I'd want shown to me, and tell you what I think even when it's not the number you were hoping for.

### Timing: season is real, but it's not everything

Winter is our high season — more buyers in town, more showings, and for many homes the strongest prices of the year. That's especially true for homes that appeal to seasonal buyers: anything near the beach, in a country club community, or that works as a second home. If you can choose your moment, listing into the season puts you in front of the biggest audience.

But don't overthink it. People buy homes here all year — relocations, job moves, and life changes don't check the calendar — and summer sellers face less competition from other listings. A well-prepared, well-priced home sells in August too. The worst timing decision is usually waiting months for the "perfect" moment while carrying costs pile up and the perfect moment turns out to look a lot like the one you already had.

### Associations and paperwork

If your home is in a community with an association — and in Boca Raton and Delray Beach, many are — there is paperwork to get in order: association documents, budgets, rules, and any pending special assessments, all of which a buyer is entitled to review. Getting those documents ready early keeps your closing from stalling later. If your home has any unpermitted work, open permits, or a known issue, tell me at the start. Surprises kill deals; disclosed facts usually just get priced.
