Every week I talk to somebody in New York or New Jersey who is done — done with the winter, done with the commute, done with paying a fortune to heat a house they barely enjoy four months a year. Most of them end up looking at the same stretch of South Florida I work in: Boca Raton, Delray Beach, and the towns around them in southern Palm Beach County. This page is the honest version of how that move usually goes, including the parts that surprise people.

### When to come down and look

The worst time to shop for a home here is from your couch in February, when everything looks perfect in photos and every listing feels urgent. The best time to look is when you can be here in person for a few days and drive the neighborhoods at different hours — morning, evening, and once in the rain, because drainage and flooding questions answer themselves fast in a South Florida downpour.

Season matters here in a way it doesn't up north. Winter is our high season: more people, more traffic, more competition for rentals, and firmer prices. Summer is hot and quiet, and sellers and landlords are often easier to deal with. If your move date is flexible, looking in the summer and moving in the fall puts you ahead of the winter crowd instead of in the middle of it.

### Rent first, or buy right away?

I tell most people the same thing: if you don't already know these towns block by block, [rent for a stretch first](/guides/renting-a-home-in-boca-raton-and-delray-beach). An annual rental gives you a year to learn which side of town fits your life — near the beach or out west, in a gated community or in a regular neighborhood, close to Atlantic Avenue or close to the Turnpike. Buying the wrong house in the wrong neighborhood is an expensive lesson. Renting the wrong apartment for a year is just a year.

Renting first also slows the whole thing down in a good way. You get a Florida driver's license, you learn what insurance and association fees really look like, and you stop comparing every price to what a house costs in New Jersey, which is a comparison that will make you miserable and doesn't help.

That said, some people should buy right away: if you've been visiting the same town for years, if you know exactly which community you want, or if you're buying something you found that doesn't come up often. The answer depends on how well you know the area, not on the market.

### What surprises people

**The heat has a schedule.** Summer here is not like a hot week in New York. It's months of it, and your life adjusts — errands in the morning, the beach early or late, and a real appreciation for a covered patio and a pool.

**Homeowner's insurance is a real budget item.** It's higher here than up north, it varies a lot from house to house, and on an older home it can be the thing that kills a deal. Ask about insurance early, not after you're in love with the kitchen. [What it costs to live in Palm Beach County](/guides/what-it-costs-to-live-in-palm-beach-county) walks through that monthly picture.

**Associations run a lot of neighborhoods.** Many communities here have a homeowners association with an application, an approval process, rules about everything from your roof color to whether you can park a truck in the driveway, and monthly or quarterly fees on top of your mortgage. None of that is bad — it's why these communities look the way they do — but it surprises people who've never lived under one.

**Florida has no state income tax, but that doesn't make it free.** Property taxes, insurance, and association fees take a real bite. Run the full monthly number, not just the mortgage.

**Distances are not what they look like on the map.** A house that's "ten minutes from the beach" in July traffic can be twenty-five minutes in February. Live here a little and you learn which roads to avoid and when.
