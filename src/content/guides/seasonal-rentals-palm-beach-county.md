Every year, from roughly the holidays through Easter, Palm Beach County fills up with people escaping the winter up north. Boca Raton, Delray Beach, Highland Beach, and the towns around them run on a seasonal rhythm, and the rental market is where you feel it most. If you're thinking about renting a place for the season, this page explains how that market actually works — because it does not work like renting anywhere else, or like renting here in July.

### What "season" means

Season is the stretch of winter when the weather here is at its best and the population swells. In practice, the heart of it runs from around January through March or April, with December and the holiday weeks busy on the front end. Seasonal rentals are typically rented by the month, with a minimum stay — many owners and associations require a minimum of one to three months, and some communities allow only one rental per year. So the first question on any seasonal rental isn't the price. It's whether the owner's terms and the community's rules allow the stay you want.

### Seasonal prices are not annual prices divided by twelve

This is the part that surprises people. A home or condo that rents for one amount on an annual lease will rent for considerably more per month during season. In the prime winter months, monthly seasonal rents on desirable properties can run two or three times what the same place would average out to over a full year. You're paying for the best weeks of the Florida year, in a furnished home, for a short stay — and so is everyone else competing for it.

The flip side is real too: if your dates are flexible, the shoulder months and the summer are a different market entirely, with more availability and softer prices. A renter who can come in November or stay into May has more choices and more negotiating room than one who needs February, which is the single hardest month to land.

### Everything is furnished — and that matters

Seasonal rentals come furnished, down to the dishes and linens. That makes them easy to move into — bring a suitcase and a toothbrush — but it also means no two are alike, and photos matter a great deal. One owner's "fully furnished" is a genuinely comfortable home; another's is the furniture they didn't want anymore. Ask what's included: utilities, internet, cable, pool service, and whether there's a cap on electric, which some owners set. Get the answers in writing in the lease, so there's nothing to argue about in February.

### Why booking early matters

The good seasonal rentals do not wait around. Repeat renters are the backbone of this market — people who rent the same place every winter often rebook for the following year before they leave, sometimes a full year ahead. Owners with a place that rents well know their calendar fills, and they have no reason to discount in the fall for a renter who shows up in December.

In practice, that means the search for next season starts in the spring and summer, not the fall. By the time the weather turns cold up north and everybody starts looking at once, the best places near the beach and in the popular communities are already spoken for, and what's left is what's left. If you're reading this in the fall for the coming winter, don't panic — places do open up when plans change, and I work with those openings all the time. Just know you're shopping the remainder, and be ready to move quickly when something right appears.

### Associations are part of the process

Many condos and communities require an application and approval for renters, seasonal renters included. That takes time — sometimes a few weeks — and it can involve a fee and a background check. It means a seasonal rental is rarely something you can arrange at the last minute, even if a unit is sitting empty. Factor the approval timeline into your plans, especially if your arrival date is fixed.

### A word about the listings you see online

If you're browsing rental listings and a beautiful furnished place near the beach has been sitting there at a great price for weeks, be careful. In this market, a lot of what's posted on the big sites has already been rented — sometimes a week or more ago — and the post just hasn't come down. The renters who do best work from what's actually available today, on the MLS, rather than chasing posts that lead to "sorry, that one just rented." That's most of the job I do for seasonal renters: cutting the dead listings away so you only spend time on real ones.

### The short version

Seasonal renting here rewards the early and the ready. Know your dates, expect to pay a premium for the prime months, confirm what's furnished and what's included, leave time for association approval, and start earlier than you think you need to. Do that, and winter in Palm Beach County is exactly as good as it looks from up north in January.
